<CaseSection title="The problem">
  <Prose>
    Every client campaign ran on just two hardcoded templates. Any time marketing needed a change, a developer had to step in — which meant delays and friction. Marketing wanted flexibility; the product team wanted faster launches. The two pulled against each other, and neither could scale.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Approach">
  <Prose>
    I started with the pages we already had, looking for the patterns and layouts that performed, and talked to marketing and production about which content they actually used and which they never touched.

    Then, working with engineering, I built reusable blocks in Figma aligned to shadcn/ui and Tailwind, on a token system that standardized spacing, colour, and typography. It shipped incrementally — real campaigns drove the improvements.

    <PullQuote>Rather than rigid templates, pages are built from predefined, themeable blocks — flexibility without a design compromise.</PullQuote>

    The library covers the whole page: hero, CTA, price grid, extra prizes, winners, image galleries, checkout, and more. Each block is variable-powered, so colour, spacing, and type flex per campaign without anyone editing code.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Outcomes">
  <Outcomes>
    <Outcome period="Launch time" title="Faster campaigns" description="Pages build quickly, with far less back-and-forth with the production team." />
    <Outcome period="Clients" title="Happier" description="More customization means each campaign fits the charity behind it." />
    <Outcome period="Engineering" title="Less strain" description="Engineers work on system-wide improvements instead of one-off requests." />
  </Outcomes>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="What's next">
  <Prose>
    Wire in performance analytics for data-driven A/B testing, extend the system with new layouts as campaigns teach us what works, and document it so internal teams can adopt it end to end.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>
