<Prose>
  VanHack had always acquired customers through sales, but we were testing whether the product itself could create enough value upfront to support a self-serve subscription model. 
  
  The existing onboarding asked first-time employers to complete a 7-step, 11-question job-posting form before seeing useful candidate matches, so many users entered fake data just to access the product. 
  
  I redesigned the flow around speed to value and progressive commitment: employers could start exploring candidates immediately, build a role through their search behavior, and sign up only when they had something worth saving. 
  
  The outcome was an end-to-end clickable prototype and design-system handoff to help the team validate whether product-led onboarding could become a new revenue stream without hurting the existing sales motion.
</Prose>

<CaseSection title="The current state">
  <Prose>
    In the current onboarding, a first-time employer completed a full 7-step, 11-question job-posting form before starting to see some candidates that would fit. Most first-timers filled the form with fake data to see the real product before committing.

<CaseSection title="Design approach">
  <Prose>
    The design was based on two principles: **speed to value**, and **progressive commitment**. Users won't invest effort until they've seen value.
  </Prose>

  <SubHeading>Design principles</SubHeading>

  <Prose>
    The search starts on the website, at zero commitment. A public talent pool makes **all interactions lead toward a higher goal.**

    A self-building role card that creates the job description from what the user searches, refines, and shortlists, **replacing the long intake form with the act of exploring itself.**

    An AI agent fills the gaps one question at a time, **reducing the perceived effort of filling a form.** It just feels like answering questions to improve the results.

    A commitment ladder that asks for signup only to **preserve work the user already built**, and for payment only at the moment of interviewing.
