<Prose>
  For ten years, every VanHack customer was won over the same way: by a person. A salesperson found, pitched, and closed them. So, what if the product closed the deal instead? We're testing whether a self-serve subscription could open a new revenue stream, product-led rather than sales-led.
</Prose>

<CaseSection title="Project Kickoff: The new onboarding flow">
  <Prose>
    The idea of improving the onboarding experience while testing a subscription revenue stream started after research showed that this could work for us. AI-driven companies are now operating on this model, and the market confirmed that. Sales included the new model in their pitch, and companies started to adopt it. So, the next question is: could the product show enough value up front to make the self-serve subscription possible, without hurting the sales motion that closes deals today?

    To test it, we decided to take advantage of the fact the website was already being revamped and the tech team was restructuring the platform to rethink the onboarding flow alongside it.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="The current state">
  <Prose>
    In the current onboarding, a first-time employer completed a full 7-step, 11-question job-posting form before starting to see some candidates that would fit. Most first-timers filled the form with fake data to see the real product before committing.

    After all the steps, users were still seeing a non-interactive list of candidates and a call to action to book a call with a sales representative. That means a dead end for the product experience.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Design approach">
  <Prose>
    The design was based on two principles: **speed to value**, and **progressive commitment**. Users won't invest effort until they've seen value.
  </Prose>

  <SubHeading>Design principles</SubHeading>

  <Prose>
    The search starts on the website, at zero commitment. A public talent pool makes **all interactions lead toward a higher goal.**

    A self-building role card that create the job description from what the user searches, refines, and shortlists, **replacing the long intake form with the act of exploring itself.**

    An AI agent fills the gaps one question at a time, **reducing the perceived effort of filling a form.** It just feels like answering questions to improve the results.

    A commitment ladder that asks for signup only to **preserve work the user already built**, and for payment only at the moment of interviewing.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>
