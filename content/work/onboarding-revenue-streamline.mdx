<CaseSection title="A new way in">
  <Prose>
    For ten years, every VanHack customer was won the same way — by a person. A salesperson found them, pitched them, and closed them. The question we set out to test was whether self-serve subscription could open a new revenue stream: a product-led alternative to sales-led acquisition.

    <PullQuote>Users won't invest effort until they've seen value.</PullQuote>

    So the whole redesign is built to show value first, and ask for commitment last.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="The current state">
  <Prose>
    Employers filled out a seven-step, eleven-question form before they saw a single candidate match. Most people typed fake data just to get a preview, and the flow ended on a non-interactive candidate list and a "book a call" button — the opposite of a product that sells itself.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Design approach">
  <Prose>
    I built the flow around two principles: speed to value, and progressive commitment.

    **Search before signup.** The public talent pool is searchable straight from the marketing site, at zero commitment.

    **A self-building role card.** Instead of a long intake form, the role assembles itself as you go, and an AI agent answers questions incrementally.

    **A commitment ladder.** Signup and payment are deferred until the employer has already seen candidates worth paying for.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="What we're measuring">
  <Prose>
    The work is in progress, and I owned the full scope — problem framing, the complete flow, every screen, a clickable prototype, and a design-system handoff wired into our AI tools. These are the signals we're watching.

    **Time to first match.** How fast an employer sees a candidate who fits, before any signup.

    **Signup-to-shortlist without a card.** Whether people can get to real value with no payment details.

    **Trial-to-paid conversion.** How the two-path funnel converts across self-serve and sales.

    **Revenue closed through the product.** The point of the whole exercise.
  </Prose>
</CaseSection>
